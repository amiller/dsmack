App Attest on chain

What it takes to verify an Apple App Attest attestation in a smart contract instead of on the app developer's server. The page maps Apple's validation steps to the dsmack verifier contracts, re-runs the key checks on a live Mac capture in your browser, and keeps the reference values in one place.

Source: amiller/dsmack @ ae28913 Apple: Validating apps that connect to your server

Four transactions

Apple's attestation object is a CBOR map with a certificate chain, a receipt and authenticator data. The contracts never parse the CBOR. A client splits out the leaf certificate, the authData and the raw clientData, and the intermediate certificate is verified once, separately, then referred to by an entry id. Gas figures are from the live run on Base Sepolia on 21 October 2026.

Two enrollment paths accept the same attestations. enroll does everything in Solidity. enrollLeafProof takes an UltraHonk proof that the Noir leaf circuit ran the same leaf checks, plus 24 public fields the adapter compares against policy; the live leaf proof took 13.9 s and 2.97 GB on a 12-core Linux box.

What the contracts add, and what they skip

Checked beyond Apple's list

  • Strict DER. Exact algorithm identifiers, v3 only, minimal non-negative integers, scalars in range, UTCTime profile, no trailing bytes, at most 16 extensions with no duplicates. Unknown extensions must be non-critical.
  • Leaf extension values. basicConstraints 3000, keyUsage 030204f0, and the Apple EKU 1.2.840.113635.100.4.24, all byte-exact.
  • Mac key ACL (OID 1.2.840.113635.100.8.6). The value must equal Apple's documented aclBlob for "SIP and full security mode enabled", outer wrapper included.
  • CDHash. apple_cd_hash_hash_01 must be present, 32 bytes, type SHA-256, and admitted by the adapter's policy, on enrollment and on every assertion.
  • Validation category (apple_validation_category_01) must equal the adapter's pin: 3, Apple Development, for the deployed adapters.
  • authData shape. ED flag required, reserved bits zero, byte-exact COSE key, canonical CBOR with a closed set of extension keys.
  • Time. Leaf, intermediate and root validity against block.timestamp. A key's expiry is the earlier of the leaf's and the intermediate's notAfter.

Not checked

  • OID 1.2.840.113635.100.8.7 (OS version, build, platform). Present in every leaf and skipped as a non-critical extension.
  • OID 1.2.840.113635.100.8.5 (holds the App ID string among other fields). Skipped; the App ID is bound through the RP ID hash instead.
  • apple_bundle_version_01. Parsed and allowed, never compared. Apple says to check it on assertions. Mac captures don't carry it; the iPhone capture does.
  • The receipt. It is for Apple's fraud-metric service and never reaches the chain.
  • Low-s. High-s leaf signatures are accepted (the circuit normalizes to match). Assertions use the precompile, which doesn't enforce low-s either.
  • Intermediate issuer name and root expiry at endorsement time. Only the root signature binds the intermediate; root expiry is enforced later, in enroll and verify.
  • The measure offsets in CDRegistry aren't checked to point at __LINKEDIT and LC_CODE_SIGNATURE. Pass cd_args.py output verbatim.

Assertions

verify(context, abi.encode(keyId, authData, r, s)) can be called only by the adapter's registry address. context is the clientDataHash the app passed to generateAssertion; the fixtures store its preimage, a short JSON string, and the caller hashes it. The contract checks the key is enrolled and unexpired, runs the same authData rules without the attested-credential part, re-admits the CDHash, requires the counter to go up, then calls P256VERIFY at 0x100 with sha256(sha256(authData ‖ context)). The live run replayed assertion a1 and got assertion replay.

Because admission runs on every assertion, the registry adapter tracks the code that is approved now. Approving a new build with setBuild makes earlier CDHashes stop resolving, and keys enrolled under them stop verifying. A renamed and re-signed copy of the approved build is admitted through the open registerBuild without an owner transaction.

Apple's own test vector

Apple publishes one worked iOS attestation in its Attestation Object Validation Guide. It passes our strict-DER leaf parse, the iOS ACL check and the P-384 signature under CA 1. enroll rejects it at three points:

  • certificate nonce: the sample's nonce is sha256(authData ‖ raw challenge), while the guide's text says sha256(authData ‖ clientDataHash).
  • auth flags: flags are 0x40, without the ED bit, although an extensions map follows.
  • missing extension: no apple_cd_hash_* entries. This one is our CDHash policy, which Apple doesn't require.

The first two look like errors in Apple's sample. Others have reported them in Apple developer forums thread 842509, along with a wrong public key hash in the guide's text and a bundle version shown as "1.0" that is "1" in the data.

Other verifiers on the same captures

We ran three open-source App Attest verifiers on our five captures (three Mac, one iPhone, one Developer ID Mac), with each library's clock set inside the leaf's three-day validity window. None accepts current macOS 27 or iOS 27 output as published.

With two diagnostic workarounds (clearing AT on a parse copy for webauthn4j; an 8-line patch adding the two CDHash keys to takimoto3), both libraries accept every capture our contract accepts and agree with it on chain, nonce, key id, credentialId, RP ID hash, AAGUID, counter and signature. They are looser in four places: neither re-checks the CDHash on assertions, so assertions from a modified build pass; neither compares the authData key with the certificate key; neither stops accepting assertions when the leaf expires; and webauthn4j checks no ACL, category or CDHash. takimoto3 checks the Mac aclBlob (same value as ours) only through an unexported function, and reports the category without enforcing it.

One intermediate, many samples

Apple's PKI pages list the App Attestation root but no intermediate. We decoded about 60 distinct real attestations published in GitHub repos, from August 2020 to October 2026, iOS 14.0 to 27.2 and macOS 27.0 and 27.0.1, development and production. Every one is issued by Apple App Attestation CA 1, serial 09bac5e1…0854, including Apple's own 2026 sample. A search for "App Attestation CA 2" finds nothing. CA 1 expires on 13 March 2030 and Apple has said nothing about a successor. Leaf certificates used to be valid for 9 to 12 months and have been valid for 3 days since late 2025, so a successor would have to appear before leaves start outliving CA 1. The contract handles that with one more endorseIntermediate transaction.

The receipt has its own chain: Application Attestation Fraud Receipt Signing (valid about 13 months) → Apple Application Integration CA 5 - G1 → Apple Root CA - G3. The contracts don't use it.

Public macOS attestations: arkavo-org/authnz-rs (27.0, category 3), hellas-ai/hellas (27.0 beta, category 6 with CDHash, one assertion), Augustas11/macprovider (27.0.1, no extensions, two assertions) and amiller/abalone (27.0, category 3 with CDHash). iOS 27 samples are in webauthn4j issue 1497, webauthn4j's test resources, glincker/theauth and procivis/one-core. Older iOS fixture sets with assertions are in veehaitch/devicecheck-appattest, iansampson/AppAttest, appattest-rs and warden-supreme.

Shapes seen in those samples that a strict parser has to decide about: the ED flag is 0x40 in Apple's sample and in the macOS 27.0.1 sample but 0xc0 elsewhere; iOS 27 assertions set AT with no credential data (99 bytes); and the macOS 27.0.1 sample has no extensions even though its app opts in to the CDHash. Our contract requires ED and the CDHash, so it would reject the last one.

Open questions

  • Does macOS 27.0.1 still put the CDHash in authData? The one public 27.0.1 sample has no extensions at all, not even the category. Its app declares the opt-in entitlement as the string "CDhash", where ours uses the array ["CDhash"], so the entitlement may be the cause rather than the OS. Our captures are 27.0.
  • The 8.7 sub-tags. Apple names 8.7 only as DeviceAttestationDeviceOSInformation, in xnu's CoreTrust header. Reverse engineering gives [1400] OS version, [1403] build, [1026] platform and [1029] build variant ("Beta", "Internal"); [1104], [1401] and [1418]–[1420] are unconfirmed.
  • 8.5 (DeviceAttestationKeyUsageProperties): [1204] is the App ID; [4] is 10 on every iOS sample and 13 on every Mac; the rest are unknown.
  • Whether to enforce apple_bundle_version_01 on assertions. Apple says to verify it and gives no comparison rule; every sample carries "1", Xcode's default CFBundleVersion. Mac captures don't carry it.
  • Whether to require 8.7. One reverse-engineering report says the device chooses which extensions go into the leaf.
  • A Developer ID build (category 6) needs its own adapter.
