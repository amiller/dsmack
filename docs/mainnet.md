# Dsmack mainnet — planned

There is no recommended Dsmack P2P mainnet release or mainnet invitation yet.
The [public testnet joining page](join-testnet.md) is the entry point for peer
participation; check its availability before installing. A coupon service using
Base mainnet is a separate deployment, not a Dsmack P2P mainnet launch.

## Launch criteria

Mainnet needs a separately reviewed release decision with evidence for:

- Independent friends installing the recommended signed build, joining,
  synchronizing, restarting and upgrading while preserving their accounts.
- A tested update policy: recommended and deprecated releases, an announced
  admission cutoff, failed-update recovery and an operator recovery procedure.
- Observable node versions, fresh check-ins and scoped synchronization evidence;
  stale records must not appear online or synchronized.
- Published protocol and admission rules, operator powers, supported platforms,
  outage handling and a defined service/support window.
- Security review of the selected custody, rollback, account recovery and
  privileged-control model, with unresolved limitations visible to participants.
- Separate authorization and qualification for any real-money accounting,
  funding or coupon service. Peer membership does not enable issuance by itself.
- An explicit membership-abuse policy, with tested claims about identity rather
  than an assumed one-device or one-person guarantee.

No mainnet date, token value, airdrop or testnet-to-mainnet entitlement is promised.
Network identifiers, contracts and a recommended release will be published here
only after qualification. Testnet accounts are not promised automatic migration.

## Device identity and Sybil resistance

An account, an attested membership key, a physical device and a person are
different identities. Current participation receipts do not prove a unique Mac
or person. Keeping an account across upgrades establishes continuity, not scarcity.

Secure hardware and attestation can contribute key protection and admission
evidence under a particular profile. They do not, by themselves, establish a
network-wide limit on memberships. A device-uniqueness proposal must specify the
issuer and scope of its identifier, privacy/linkability, duplicate enrollment,
reinstallation, key rotation, device reset or resale, recovery and revocation.
Those cases need adversarial acceptance tests before a uniqueness claim is made.
Even a proven one-membership-per-device rule permits one person with many devices.

Mainnet's Sybil-resistance mechanism is undecided. Until it is reviewed and
qualified, neither participant counts nor receipts should be interpreted as
counts of independent people or used as a proven scarce identity for rewards.
