Permissionless development · Verifiable execution

An open market
for development work.

Fund a task. Let independent peers contribute code. Use App Attest evidence and published acceptance rules to make rewards claimable through a smart contract.

Dsmack is building the execution layer for that market: workers use their own Macs and coding agents, and submit evidence another party can verify—without needing a trusted relationship with the task creator.

From a funded task to a claimed reward

Post it. Build it. Prove it. Claim it.

The task creator defines what counts as success. Workers choose which tasks to attempt. The contract holds the reward until a submission satisfies its settlement rules.

  1. 01

    Post & fund

    Publish the repository, task, acceptance checks and reward. Deposit the funds into a smart contract.

  2. 02

    Workers build

    Independent peers pick up the task and use their Macs and coding agents to produce a candidate.

  3. 03

    Submit work & evidence

    Run the pinned acceptance checks. Submit the candidate and App Attest evidence binding the selected execution and result.

  4. 04

    Claim the reward

    The contract validates the required evidence and task bindings. A valid claim releases the funds to the worker.

The intended marketplace flow. The current runner alpha demonstrates attested execution and full result verification; connecting this release to the complete permissionless claim-and-payment flow remains integration work.

For task creators

Open the work
to more people.

Offer a reward for a fix, feature or testable milestone. Publish the exact acceptance routine and environment so contributors know what they are attempting before spending time or model credits.

A passing test suite, browser-driven integration checks, or a pinned agent review can be part of the task’s success definition. The quality of those rules determines what you are buying.

For workers

Bring your Mac.
Choose your work.

Inspect a task’s requirements, reward and costs. Run your own coding agent, contribute a candidate, then use the admitted runner to execute the required checks and retain the evidence.

Submit against the published terms. In the intended market, participation and claim validation are protocol operations—neither requires a human gatekeeper to approve you as a worker.

Why App Attest matters

A submission carries more than “it passed on my machine.”

Apple App Attest authenticates an admitted app’s key and assertions. Dsmack’s runner binds those assertions to the selected execution and result. A verifier checks those bindings against the task’s expected environment, checker and outcome.

Task & candidate + Runner & environment + Checks & result → Verifiable submission

The contract’s rules bind each claim to the task and the worker’s reward address, and prevent the same evidence from being used to claim twice. Workers submit evidence; the protocol checks it.

App Attest does not decide whether code is useful. The published acceptance routine defines success. The full runner/result verifier must check the execution evidence; an app assertion or a host-written “passed” file alone is insufficient.
